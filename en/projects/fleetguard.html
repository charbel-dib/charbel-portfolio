Personal project · Applied ML · 2026

FleetGuard.

Classify APS failures while accounting for the cost of errors.

On the public Scania APS (Air Pressure System) dataset, classify failures and choose a threshold based on error costs, then make the decision inspectable in an application.

01

What the project delivers

On the 16,000-row official test: 95.73% recall and 43.05% precision. 359 APS cases detected out of 375; 16 missed failures and 475 false alarms. Cost: 10 × 475 + 500 × 16 = 12,750 challenge units, not euros saved. Results recorded in the repository’s model card.

02

The problem

On an imbalanced dataset, high accuracy can hide the most costly failures. The Scania challenge assigns a cost of 10 to a false alarm and 500 to a missed APS failure. That trade-off should drive the decision.

03

Technical decisions

01

Evaluate before optimising

Explicit baselines, duplicate-aware group splits, and separate fitting, calibration and threshold-selection roles.

02

Freeze the entire decision

Model, preprocessing, calibration and threshold are recorded together. The official test is evaluated after that freeze.

03

Make the model usable

FastAPI service, CSV batch review and a React interface. Scores and missing values remain visible to help interpret decisions.

04

Evaluation

Preprocessing, model, calibration and threshold are frozen before the official test. Recall and precision must be read together: detecting more APS cases comes with many false alarms here.

What I take from it

Connect an ML metric to an operational decision while keeping the false-alarm trade-off visible.

05

Scope & limits

Classification on an anonymised public dataset, not a forecast of future failures. Results do not validate use on a real fleet. AWS infrastructure is prepared in the repository; the inspected report does not establish an actual AWS deployment. Hyperparameter selection and calibration are not fully independent: this protocol is not nested validation.
